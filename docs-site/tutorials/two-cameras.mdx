## What you'll do

You'll record with two cameras at once, then pick which one shows at each moment. Both cameras share one clock, so they're always in sync.

## Before you start

You need two cameras. Your Mac's built-in camera and your iPhone are a great pair. To use your iPhone, sign in to the same Apple Account on both, turn on Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, and put the locked phone near your Mac (or plug it in). See [Use your iPhone as a camera](/record/layouts-and-cameras#use-your-iphone-as-a-camera).

## Record

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the recorder">
    On the **Projects** screen, press **New recording**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick Camera A">
    In the bottom bar, the first camera menu is **Camera A** (**1** below). Pick your main camera, usually the one in front of you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick Camera B">
    Open the **B** menu next to it (**2**) and pick your second camera, like your iPhone.
    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/annotated/record-two-cameras.png" alt="The recorder with FaceTime HD Camera as camera A and iPhone Camera as camera B. Both camera bubbles show on the preview, marked 3 and 4" />
    </Frame>
    Now both cameras show on the preview (**3** and **4**).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Move a bubble or change its shape (optional)">
    Drag the camera bubble to another corner, out of the way of what you're showing. Hover over it and press the shape button to switch between rounded, circle and square.
    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/annotated/record-bubble.png" alt="The camera bubble, marked 1, with its shape button marked 2" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Press Start recording">
    Record as usual. While you record, the floating bubble shows one camera. Hover over it and press **A** or **B** to check the other one.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Edit

<Steps>
  <Step title="Watch Camera B">
    Open the **Camera** tab on the right (**Command + 3**). Under **Your two cameras**, pick **Camera B**. Now Camera B shows everywhere, so you can play through it. Pick **Camera A** to go back.
    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/annotated/two-cameras-tab.png" alt="The Camera tab, marked 1, with the Your two cameras note marked 2 and the Camera B button marked 3" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Switch cameras at one moment">
    Click the timeline where you want the other camera. Then, in the **Add** bar on the left of the preview, click **View** and choose **Other camera (A ↔ B)**. Or press **Control + A**.
    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/annotated/two-cameras-switch.png" alt="The View menu open from the Add bar, with Other camera (A ↔ B) marked 2" />
    </Frame>
    The other camera shows for 4 seconds from there.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make it longer or shorter">
    Switch on **Advanced** above the timeline, then set it to **3 visible timelines**. Each view is a block on its own row. Drag its ends, or click it and pick **Camera A** or **Camera B** under **Camera angle**.
    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/annotated/two-cameras-timeline.png" alt="The advanced timeline with the Advanced switch marked 1, 3 visible timelines marked 2, and a Screen + camera B block marked 3. The panel on the right shows Camera angle with Camera B picked" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Cuts always apply to both cameras and your voice at once. You never have to line anything up.
</Tip>

Learn more in [Edit with two cameras](/edit/two-cameras).
